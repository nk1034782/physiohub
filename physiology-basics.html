01

Cells & Homeostasis

🧫

Cell Membrane

The cell membrane separates intracellular and extracellular environments and controls movement of substances.

↔️

Transport

Simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis and active transport are key mechanisms.

⚖️

Homeostasis

Homeostasis regulates internal conditions around suitable ranges using feedback systems.

02

Nervous System Physiology

🧠

Neurons

Neurons receive and transmit information using electrical and chemical signals.

⚡

Synapses

Chemical synapses use neurotransmitters to influence the next cell.

🎯

Motor Control

The nervous system coordinates sensory information and motor output for posture and purposeful movement.

03

Cardiovascular Physiology

❤️

Heart

The heart pumps blood through pulmonary and systemic circulations.

📈

Cardiac Output

Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume.

🩸

Blood Pressure

Blood pressure is influenced by cardiac output and vascular resistance.

04

Respiratory Physiology

🫁

Ventilation

Ventilation is movement of air into and out of the lungs, driven by pressure changes.

🔄

Gas Exchange

Oxygen and carbon dioxide move between alveolar air and pulmonary blood across the respiratory membrane.

🩸

Oxygen Transport

Most oxygen is transported bound to haemoglobin; carbon dioxide is transported in several forms, mainly bicarbonate.

05

Muscle Physiology

💪

Contraction

Skeletal muscle contraction involves actin and myosin interaction, regulated by calcium and powered by ATP.

⚡

Motor Units

A motor unit consists of a motor neuron and the skeletal muscle fibres it controls.

🔋

Fatigue

Muscle performance can decline during sustained or repeated activity because of multiple physiological factors.

06

Renal & Fluid Balance

💧

Kidneys

Kidneys filter blood and regulate water, electrolytes, acid-base balance and contribute to blood-pressure regulation.

🧪

Urine Formation

Filtration, reabsorption and secretion occur within the nephron.

⚖️

Fluid Balance

Fluid balance is influenced by intake, renal regulation and hormones such as ADH and aldosterone.
