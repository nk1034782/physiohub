01

Bones & Skeletal System

🦴

Axial Skeleton

Skull, vertebral column, ribs and sternum form the main axial framework.

🦵

Appendicular Skeleton

Shoulder girdle and upper limb, plus pelvic girdle and lower limb.

📌

Bone Types

Long, short, flat and irregular bones are common structural classifications.

02

Joints & Movements

🔗

Joint Types

Fibrous, cartilaginous and synovial joints are the broad structural groups.

↔️

Common Movements

Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction and rotation are common movements.

🦿

Examples

Shoulder and hip are ball-and-socket joints; elbow is primarily a hinge joint; knee is a modified hinge.

03

Muscles

💪

Muscle Types

Skeletal muscle produces voluntary movement; cardiac muscle forms the heart; smooth muscle is found in internal organs and vessels.

🏋️

Major Groups

Rotator cuff, chest, back, abdominal, gluteal, quadriceps, hamstrings and calf groups are useful study areas.

📖

How to Study

For each muscle learn location, origin, insertion, action and nerve supply, then relate it to functional movement.

04

Nervous System Anatomy

🧠

Central Nervous System

Brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system.

⚡

Peripheral Nervous System

Nerves outside the brain and spinal cord connect the CNS with the body.

🩺

Physiotherapy Relevance

Basic nerve anatomy supports assessment of sensation, motor function, reflexes and movement.

05

Major Body Regions

👤

Head & Neck

Skull, cervical spine and major muscles and nerves.

💪

Upper Limb

Shoulder, arm, elbow, forearm, wrist and hand.

🦵

Lower Limb

Hip, thigh, knee, leg, ankle and foot.

🫁

Trunk

Thorax, abdomen, pelvis and vertebral column.
