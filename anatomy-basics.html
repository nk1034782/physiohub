Anatomy Basics

A simple introduction to anatomical position, planes, axes, directional terms and basic body movements for physiotherapy students.

🎯 Learning Objectives

After studying this page, you should be able to:

  • Describe the standard anatomical position.
  • Identify the major anatomical planes.
  • Understand common directional terms.
  • Understand the relationship between planes and axes.
  • Recognise common body movements.

🧍 Anatomical Position

The standard reference position used when describing locations and movements of the body.

Key Features

  • Body standing upright.
  • Head and eyes facing forward.
  • Upper limbs positioned beside the body.
  • Palms facing forward.
  • Lower limbs positioned beside each other.
  • Feet directed forward.

📐 Anatomical Planes

Planes are imaginary surfaces used to describe sections and movements of the body.

Sagittal Plane

Divides the body into right and left portions.

Frontal Plane

Divides the body into anterior and posterior portions.

Transverse Plane

Divides the body into superior and inferior portions.

Oblique Plane

Passes through the body at an angle.

🔄 Anatomical Axes

Axes are imaginary lines around which movement can occur.

Axis General Direction Example Movement
Vertical / Longitudinal Superior to inferior Rotation
Medio-lateral Side to side Flexion / Extension
Antero-posterior Front to back Abduction / Adduction

🧭 Directional Terms

Important terminology used to describe anatomical location.

Term Meaning
Superior Toward the head or upper part
Inferior Toward the feet or lower part
Anterior Toward the front
Posterior Toward the back
Medial Toward the midline
Lateral Away from the midline
Proximal Closer to the trunk or point of origin
Distal Farther from the trunk or point of origin
Superficial Closer to the surface
Deep Farther from the surface

🏃 Basic Body Movements

Common movements frequently used in physiotherapy.

Flexion

A movement that generally decreases the angle between two body segments.

Extension

A movement that generally increases the angle between two body segments.

Abduction

Movement away from the body's midline.

Adduction

Movement toward the body's midline.

Internal Rotation

Rotation of a body segment toward the midline.

External Rotation

Rotation of a body segment away from the midline.

Pronation

A rotational movement of the forearm that turns the palm posteriorly in anatomical reference.

Supination

A rotational movement of the forearm that turns the palm anteriorly in anatomical reference.

🩺 Clinical Connection

Why anatomy terminology matters in physiotherapy.

Clear anatomical terminology helps physiotherapists describe patient findings, document assessments, communicate with other healthcare professionals and describe therapeutic movements accurately.

📝 Anatomy MCQs

Test your knowledge.

1. Which plane divides the body into right and left portions?

Answer: B — Sagittal plane.

2. Movement away from the body's midline is called:

Answer: C — Abduction.

3. Which term means closer to the trunk or point of origin?

Answer: B — Proximal.

4. Which plane divides the body into superior and inferior portions?

Answer: C — Transverse plane.

5. In anatomical position, the palms face:

Answer: B — Anteriorly.

🗣️ Anatomy Viva Questions

Short questions for viva and revision practice.

1. What is anatomical position?

The standard reference position used to describe the location and movement of body structures.

2. Name the three major anatomical planes.

Sagittal, frontal and transverse planes.

3. What is medial?

A position or direction toward the body's midline.

4. What is proximal?

A position closer to the trunk or point of origin.

5. What is abduction?

Movement away from the body's midline.
